## Executive Summary

To evaluate whether the 6-arc-second (~180 m) terrain grid utilized in Phase 3D.3 compromises the geographical or hydrological integrity of the Hathnikund upstream catchment, an extensive multi-resolution and multi-resampling benchmark suite was executed on the authentic Copernicus GLO-30 DEM mosaic (7,200 × 7,200 cells at 1 arc-second).

The experiment evaluated 6 distinct terrain configurations across three spatial resolutions (12", 6", and 3") and four downsampling methods (nearest-neighbour decimation, bilinear interpolation, area-weighted average, and thalweg-preserving block minimum aggregation).

### Key Empirical Findings:
1. **Downsampling Nature:** The original Phase 3D.3 processing pipeline utilized **point decimation via nearest-neighbour sampling** centered at pixel offset `(3, 3)` (sampling 1 pixel out of every 36 and discarding 35).
2. **Boundary Stability:** Across all resolutions and resampling methods (a 16-fold increase in grid density from 12" to 3"), the delineated watershed area varies by less than **0.17%** (spanning $11,355.18\text{ km}^2$ to $11,374.91\text{ km}^2$), and spatial Intersection over Union (IoU) remains $\ge 99.44\%$.
3. **Resampling Invariance:** Comparing the 6" nearest-neighbour baseline against area-weighted average and bilinear resampling yielded an IoU of **99.89%** and **99.88%** respectively, with less than $1.4\text{ km}^2$ ($+0.01\%$) area difference.
4. **Hydrological Connectivity:** Priority-Flood conditioning coupled with Breadth-First Search (BFS) flat routing achieved 100% depression resolution and acyclic flow enforcement across all configurations, maintaining topological continuity for all five major Himalayan tributaries (Upper Yamuna, Tons, Giri, Pabbar, and Asan).

## 6. Exact DEM Resampling Implementations

The exact method used to ingest and resample the raw 1-arc-second Copernicus DEM mosaic (`upstream_yamuna_dem_mosaic.tif`) for each experiment is documented below:

### Experiments 1, 2, 6: Nearest-Neighbour Decimated Read
```python
# Executed via Rasterio / GDAL dataset reading
with rasterio.open(mosaic_path) as src:
  target_h = src.height // step
  target_w = src.width // step
  trans = src.transform * Affine.scale(step, step)
  dem = src.read(
      1, out_shape=(target_h, target_w), resampling=Resampling.nearest
  ).astype(np.float32)
```
* **Step 12 (`12_arcsec_nearest`):** Target shape `(600, 600)`. Point sampled at center offset $(12r + 6, 12c + 6)$.
* **Step 6 (`6_arcsec_nearest_baseline`):** Target shape `(1200, 1200)`. Point sampled at center offset $(6r + 3, 6c + 3)$.
* **Step 3 (`3_arcsec_nearest`):** Target shape `(2400, 2400)`. Point sampled at center offset $(3r + 1.5, 3c + 1.5)$.

### Experiment 3: Bilinear Interpolation
```python
with rasterio.open(mosaic_path) as src:
  dem = src.read(
      1, out_shape=(1200, 1200), resampling=Resampling.bilinear
  ).astype(np.float32)
```
* Resamples the elevation field using bi-linear weighted interpolation of the four nearest 1-arc-second grid nodes, smoothing local cliff and canyon wall micro-roughness.

### Experiment 4: Area-Weighted Average Resampling
```python
with rasterio.open(mosaic_path) as src:
  dem = src.read(1, out_shape=(1200, 1200), resampling=Resampling.average).astype(
      np.float32
  )
```
* Computes the arithmetic mean elevation across all 36 original 1-arc-second pixels contributing to each 6-arc-second grid cell.

### Experiment 5: Thalweg-Preserving Block Minimum Aggregation
```python
with rasterio.open(mosaic_path) as src:
  full_dem = src.read(1).astype(np.float32)
  target_h = src.height // 6
  target_w = src.width // 6
  h, w = target_h * 6, target_w * 6
  # Reshape into (1200, 6, 1200, 6) blocks and calculate block minimum
  dem = full_dem[:h, :w].reshape(target_h, 6, target_w, 6).min(axis=(1, 3))
```
* Preserves the lowest point within each $6 \times 6$ block, guaranteeing that narrow canyon riverbeds (thalwegs) are captured in the conditioned surface rather than being occluded by steep valley walls.

## 7. Analysis: Pixel Decimation vs Raster Resampling in the Original DEM

### Forensic Assessment of Phase 3D.3 Implementation
In `ml/spatial/catchment.py` (lines 258–260) and `scratch/generate_authentic_watershed.py`, the DEM read call was:
```python
step = max(1, int(max(src.height, src.width) / 1200))
arr = src.read(1, out_shape=(src.height // step, src.width // step))
trans = transform * Affine.scale(step, step)
```

1. **Default Resampling Behavior:**
   When the `resampling` parameter is omitted from `src.read(..., out_shape=...)`, `rasterio` defaults to `Resampling.nearest`.
2. **Empirical Verification of Offset:**
   As demonstrated by `verify_resampling_call.py` and `verify_offset.py`:
   - `np.array_equal(arr_default, arr_nearest)` evaluates to **`True`**.
   - Direct slicing `sub_full[0::6, 0::6]` does *not* match `arr_default` because GDAL centers the resampling window at half the step size.
   - Slicing `sub_full[3::6, 3::6]` matches `arr_default` with **100% identical pixel values**.
3. **Scientific Classification:**
   The original 6-arc-second DEM was produced through **point decimation via centered nearest-neighbour sampling**, not area-weighted aggregation or bilinear interpolation. Exactly 1 pixel was sampled for every 36 source pixels, and the remaining 35 were discarded.

### Impact of Point Decimation on Hydrological Modeling:
* **Local Micro-Terrain Impact:**
  In deeply incised Himalayan V-shaped gorges (where valley widths are $<60\text{ m}$), nearest-neighbour sampling at offset $(3, 3)$ frequently lands on steep canyon valley walls rather than the riverbed. This introduced **20,066 artificial sinks** requiring depression filling across 40,589 cells (2.82% of the raster).
  By contrast, block minimum aggregation (`6_arcsec_min_aggregation`) preserved the actual valley thalwegs, slashing the sink count by **66.9%** (to 6,651 sinks) and depression filling to only 1.08% of cells.
* **Catchment Boundary Impact:**
  Because the primary watershed divides of the Upper Yamuna (the Great Himalayan crests, Dhaula Dhar, and Shivalik ridges) possess macro-scale topographic relief spanning hundreds to thousands of meters across widths of 1 to 5 km, point decimation samples the same macroscopic ridge slopes as area-averaging. Consequently, the regional boundary is **exceptionally robust** ($\ge 99.8\%$ IoU agreement with all other resampling methods).

4. **Suitability for Catchment Hydrology:** The 6-arc-second (~180 m) grid is scientifically defensible and computationally optimal for:
   - Catchment delineation and hypsometric analysis.
   - Spatial rainfall aggregation (e.g. 20-node Open-Meteo or IMD grids).
   - Semi-distributed rainfall-runoff routing (HEC-HMS or Muskingum-Cunge lumped reach routing).

### Items That Remain Unverified / Acknowledged Limitations:
1. **1-Arc-Second Delineation:** Native 1-arc-second (~30 m) delineation was not executed due to pure-Python runtime/memory constraints. The 3-arc-second (~90 m) run serves as the verified numerical asymptote.
2. **Independent Vector River Hydrography:** Official Survey of India (SOI) or Central Water Commission (CWC) digitized vector shapefiles for upstream headwaters (Tons, Giri, Pabbar, Asan) are not currently available in `data/raw/spatial/hydrography/`. Extracted stream networks are validated against Copernicus DEM valley thalwegs and literature topology, but not yet overlaid against independent state vector hydrography.
3. **Application Boundary Limits:** The 6-arc-second (~180 m) terrain grid is **unsuitable** for local hydrodynamic flood wave inundation (2D shallow water equations), levee breach mechanics, or Paonta Sahib urban inundation modeling; those applications strictly require sub-30m or LiDAR-grade digital elevation models.
